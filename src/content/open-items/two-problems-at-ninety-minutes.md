<p>The calendar commits to two easy-to-medium problems per weekday session. It has not once been tested at the stated 90 minutes.</p>
<ul>
  <li><b>26 Aug</b> — the whole session went to one carried problem. Test deferred.</li>
  <li><b>27 Aug</b> — internals only, by choice. Coding did not run at all. Deferred again.</li>
  <li><b>28 Aug</b> — a short block, started late. One medium ran; the two-problem test was <em>declined</em> rather than attempted, on the grounds that a compressed run produces a number that cannot be trusted in either direction. Moved to Monday.</li>
  <li>Three sessions of evidence that the block does not reliably hold two tracks, and still none either way on the actual commitment.</li>
  <li>The third deferral is a different kind from the first two. Days 3 and 4 ran out of time; day 5 <b>declined the measurement to protect it</b>. That is the right call and it is worth naming, because it is also the easiest reasoning to reuse indefinitely.</li>
  <li><b>31 Aug</b> — two problems <em>did</em> run, and this still was not the test. Roughly sixty minutes, two easies. Today tells you two easies fit in an hour; the commitment is two problems in ninety minutes. Useful, not the same claim.</li>
  <li>Deferred four times. The next full block should run it with <b>two mediums</b>, or the measurement keeps being approximated by something easier.</li>
  <li><b>1 Sep</b> — a full ninety-minute block, intended as the test. One problem ran, worked to completion in both directions. 3Sum was queued and never started. <b>Fifth deferral.</b></li>
  <li>Worth deciding in advance what the answer means: if two mediums do not fit in 90 minutes, the calendar changes rather than the sessions quietly overrunning. Day 4 ran past two hours.</li>
</ul>
