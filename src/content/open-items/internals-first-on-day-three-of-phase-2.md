<p>The session opened with <em>"first internal."</em> <b>It was pushed back on</b>, because it is precisely the failure mode <a href="/sessions/2026-09-13">the phase 2 decision</a> named three days earlier — and this is the record of both halves, the slip and what stopped it.</p>

<blockquote><p><b>The failure mode to watch is treating the coding track as optional.</b> Phase 1's coding was first-class on paper too and produced one topic area, because <b>internals kept winning the block</b>. A date slip is absorbed by the 12–16 week runway; a breadth slip is absorbed by nothing.</p></blockquote>

<h4>Why this was worth two minutes of argument</h4>

<ul>
  <li><b><a href="/coding/24">Merge Two Sorted Lists</a> had already slipped from Tuesday.</b> With 60 minutes available, internals-first meant it very likely slipped again.</li>
  <li><b>The pushback distinguished a real reason from a preference</b> — mid-thought on something, or a work need, would have been a reason to run internals first, and that was offered explicitly either way.</li>
  <li><b>The user switched to coding first without argument, and the problem got done.</b> Had the order held, week 4 would be <b>two problems behind rather than one</b>.</li>
</ul>

<h4>The part worth keeping, and it is not the good outcome</h4>

<p><b>The failure mode was predicted correctly at the phase boundary and appeared on day three.</b> Three days is fast enough that the prediction was not a hedge — and it means <b>it will appear again</b>, because the pull that produced it has not changed.</p>

<div class="myth" style="margin-top:14px">
  <b>What actually worked</b>
  <b>Naming it at the moment it happened</b> — not the plan text that anticipated it. The plan had already named this exact risk in writing, three days earlier, and the plan text did not stop it. The <em>interruption</em> did.
</div>

<p>That is the same shape as the two disciplines that were adopted after months of intending them: <a href="/open#timed-and-narrated-committed-day-one-never-once-done">timing</a> moved when it got <em>start the clock before reading</em>, a step you cannot reach and skip. <b>"Coding first" needs a trigger of that kind rather than a commitment</b>, and it does not have one yet — what it had today was a person to argue with.</p>

<h4>What would close it</h4>

<p><b>Not "it did not happen again."</b> It is the third day of phase 2 and coding has led every session so far, which is why this is filed as a regression against the phase-1 pattern rather than as a new gap. <b>Closes on a week of weekday sessions where the coding block runs first without it being raised</b> — or on a mechanism being written down that makes the order not a decision each morning.</p>
