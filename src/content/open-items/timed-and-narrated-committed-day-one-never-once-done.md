<h4>Why 7 Sep is the instance worth recording</h4>
<p><a href="/coding/20">Question 20</a> was the problem <b>specifically chosen</b> to exercise all three, on the session immediately after the third was adopted. None of them ran:</p>
<ul>
  <li><b>Timer</b> — not started. Still no pace data at all.</li>
  <li><b>Narration</b> — not done.</li>
  <li><b>Fake before tests</b> — not done, because no tests were written. That one has its own item and its own history; see <a href="/open#distinguishing-input">write the input that distinguishes</a>, where 2 Sep was the first unprompted success and 7 Sep is the first skip after it.</li>
</ul>

<h4>The thing that makes this different from a missed task</h4>
<p><b>They cost no extra time.</b> Starting a timer and speaking while typing are free, which means the obstacle is not the schedule — and that rules out the explanation every previous deferral has used. Compare <a href="/open#two-problems-at-ninety-minutes">the two-problem test</a>, which was retired on exactly this reasoning: five deferrals, and the honest reading was that carrying it cost a line in every session record and answered nothing.</p>

<p>The difference is that this one is <b>not</b> droppable. It is the phase-1 exit question. Retiring it would mean retiring the criterion the phase is measured by.</p>

<h4>What would actually change it</h4>
<p>An intention has now failed on the first problem after being formed. The proposal from the same session, and the thing to test on 8 Sep:</p>
<ul>
  <li><b>Start the timer before reading the problem</b>, so it is not a step that can be reached and skipped.</li>
  <li><b>Treat the fake as part of the solution</b> rather than a step after it.</li>
</ul>
<p>Closes on <b>one problem that is timed and narrated end to end</b> — not on the intention being restated. If 3Sum on 8 Sep also runs untimed and silent, the mechanism is the problem, not the discipline.</p>
