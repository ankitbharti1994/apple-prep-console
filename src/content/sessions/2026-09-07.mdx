## What a four-day gap costs

The first data on this, and the reason the recall pass leads rather than fills time. Four questions,
blank editor, no scrollback:

| Asked | Age | Result |
|---|---|---|
| What `cancel()` actually does | 1 week | **Clean** — full model, no decay |
| `@Sendable` captures — why a `var` is rejected | 1 week | **Partial** — right word, wrong reason |
| In-flight dedup — why store the *task* | 5 days | **Half** — goal right, mechanism missing |
| 424 stale `maxFreq` — why it is safe | 6 days | **Gone** — third attempt |

The pattern is not about age. `cancel()` and `@Sendable` are the same week old and came back
differently, and the difference is what kind of thing they are: **cancel() is a rule, and the
`@Sendable` answer is a mechanism with a moving part.** The rule survived the gap. The mechanism
lost its middle step — that a captured `var` is held *by reference*, which is the reason a read is
rejected and not just a write.

That is worth more than the problem that followed it, because it says what to re-pass and what to
leave alone.

## The branch that fired

The week-3 plan carried a condition: **if two or more recall answers came back broken, Wednesday
switches from the heap build to a re-pass.** Two came back broken, so it switched — Wednesday is
now `@Sendable` boxing and the dedup mechanism, roughly thirty minutes, and the heap moves to
Friday.

Worth noting that the branch existed at all. It is the first time a contingency written in advance
has been triggered by a measurement rather than by how the morning felt.
