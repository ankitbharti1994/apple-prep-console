## What the timing actually produced

Two measurements, and the first quantitative data anywhere in this record. They are recorded on
[question 21](/coding/21) rather than written down here, so the "N of M timed" counter on the
[open items page](/open#timed-and-narrated-committed-day-one-never-once-done) counts them rather
than being maintained by hand.

| Milestone | Time |
|---|---|
| Working brute-force solution | **14 min** |
| Optimal O(n²) version | **not reached in 25 min** |

**The reaction is worth more than the numbers.** Bounding the session by time was *enjoyable*. A
discipline experienced as a constraint gets skipped; one experienced as a game does not — and that
is a better predictor of tomorrow than any single measurement. The adoption problem for timing may
now be solved.

What the numbers say about the checkpoint on 13 Sep: fourteen minutes to correct-but-too-slow is a
defensible position in a real round — *provided it is announced as too slow and the optimisation
follows*. Silently submitting an O(n³) is where the round is lost, and **that is a narration failure
rather than a speed failure.** The two halves of the exit question are not independent, and only one
of them can currently be attempted.

## The bug that was not an omission

Three things were wrong with the two-pointer conversion. Two of them are steps not yet written —
neither pointer moving on a match, so it hangs; no duplicate skipping, so `[-1,-1,2]` comes out
twice. The third is a different kind of thing:

> Sum too large moved `left` **right**, toward *larger* values.

The anchor loop was right. `target = 0 - num1` was right. The shape was recognised and the rule
that governs it was asserted backwards, which is what happens when a pattern is matched rather than
reasoned about. The durable form is a sentence, not a variable name:

*The sum is too big, so I need a smaller number, and the big numbers are on the right — so the right
pointer moves.*

Said that way round it cannot invert, because the direction is derived from where the values live.
Memorising the mapping is what produced the reversal, and the mapping is the part that decays.

## Where narration stands

Twenty-one problems, none narrated. Timing was adopted the moment it was given a mechanism.
Narration has never been given one — it is still an intention, and an intention is the thing that
has now failed twenty-one times.

The proposal for the next problem, and it is deliberately small: **narrate the first five minutes
only, out loud, then stop.** A bounded version is likelier to start than an open-ended commitment,
and starting is the entire problem. Five days to the checkpoint.

## The plan was rewritten the same day, and not because of the problem

Two things forced a change to a plan that had not been touched since it was written, and only one of
them was an audit.

**The dependency came first.** Phase 3 lists *"data-structure design with concurrency follow-ups
(LRU → thread-safe)."* An LRU cache is a hash map plus a doubly linked list — and linked lists had
never been covered, with nothing in the plan scheduled to teach them. Pulling that thread produced
the audit rather than the other way round: [every problem on the board](/open#coverage-every-problem-inside-topic-1)
sits inside topic 1, and ten topics are untouched.

The original plan assumed phase 1 would ramp "easy→medium across core patterns." It produced depth
in one area instead, and **nothing caught it because depth and breadth both look like problems
getting solved.** Phase 2 was written as maintenance — one line reading *"keep coding warm"* — and
phase 3 assumed the breadth already existed. Neither phase had a slot for learning trees or graphs
from scratch.

## The part that is a bet, stated as one

Phase 2's DP track sits entirely on **a slot that has never once run.** Two build blocks have come
and gone — 29 Aug was a rest day, 5 Sep fell inside the four-day gap — and the third, on 12 Sep, has
not happened yet. So the plan depends on a commitment with a 0-for-2 record and its next attempt
still ahead of it.

The thirty extra minutes went to Saturday rather than to weekday mornings, and the reasoning matters
more than the arithmetic. **Block length was never the constraint.** Weeks 1–3 ran sessions of 90,
60, 20 and zero minutes; days were missed; the two-problem test was deferred five times and then
retired because throughput was never the bottleneck — consistency was. A 120-minute weekday block
that sometimes does not happen yields less than a 90-minute one that reliably does. Saturday was
already allocated and already unused, so committing it fixes a phase-1 failure rather than adding a
new obligation.

**Saturday 12 September is the test**, and it was already in the week-3 plan. If it runs, committing
three more is realistic. If it does not, the DP track is fiction and should be moved rather than
left standing as an intention — which is precisely what narration has been on every problem so far.

## Written down does not mean it will happen

The revision's own weakest point, and worth recording against it rather than in its favour. Phase 2's
coding items all say *"narrated."* So did phase 1's. **Writing it into a plan has not once caused it
to happen.**

The one thing that changed today argues the same way from the other direction: timing was adopted
the moment it stopped being an intention and became a rule about *when*, and that rule — *start the
clock before reading* — is now written into the phase 2 line itself. Narration still appears there
as a word in a list, exactly as it has three times before. The revision is better than what it
replaced, and it is not evidence about whether any of it runs.

Two questions on 13 September, then, rather than one: the exit test, and whether phase 2 starts on
the 14th at all.
