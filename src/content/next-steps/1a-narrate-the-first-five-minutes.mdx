The half of the <a href="/open#timed-and-narrated-committed-day-one-never-once-done">13 Sep exit question</a> with no mechanism behind it — that item's counter carries the tally. Timing was adopted the morning it stopped being an intention and became a rule about <em>when</em>: start the clock before reading. <b>Narration has never been given that</b>, and a bounded version is likelier to start than an open-ended commitment — starting is the entire problem. Five minutes out loud, then stop, on whatever the next problem is. <b>Overtaken on 13 Sep, and by more than it asked for:</b> <a href="/coding/11">the exit test</a> was narrated <em>end to end</em>, not for five minutes. The bounded version was never needed — and the mechanism that worked was not a smaller commitment but a structural one, <b>making narration the thing under test</b> rather than a step to remember mid-solve. Same shape as <em>start the clock before reading</em>: a step you cannot reach and skip. The proposal here was right about the diagnosis and wrong about the remedy.
